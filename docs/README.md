## OctoAcme Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme's project management approach is built on five core principles: **customer-first** thinking, **iterative delivery**, **clear ownership**, **data-informed decisions**, and **psychological safety**. The organization prioritizes delivering value and usability by shipping in small, testable increments while gathering feedback and metrics to inform decisions. Each project has a named Project Manager and Product Lead who own delivery coordination and outcome definition, respectively, ensuring transparent communication with stakeholders through regular updates and well-defined escalation paths.

The OctoAcme project lifecycle comprises five sequential phases: **Initiation** (defining the problem statement and stakeholders), **Planning** (scoping resources and establishing milestones), **Execution** (building, testing, and iterating), **Release** (deploying and verifying), and **Close & Retrospective** (capturing learnings). Key roles include Project Managers who coordinate schedules and risk, Product Managers who prioritize and measure outcomes, Developers who implement features with quality standards, QA/Testing teams who validate acceptance criteria, and Stakeholders who provide inputs and approvals. Communication follows a structured cadence with weekly syncs between PM and Product Manager, twice-weekly team standups, and monthly stakeholder updates.

Quality assurance is integrated throughout execution, with teams maintaining unit and integration tests, running end-to-end smoke tests before release, and performing security scanning in CI/CD pipelines. The team follows a Pull Request workflow requiring small, focused changes (≤400 lines), automated testing, and at least one approval before merging. Day-to-day execution relies on a project board (GitHub Projects) with standardized columns and a team rhythm of 15-minute daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, and milestone demos.

Risk management and communication are formalized through risk registers tracking ID, description, impact, likelihood, owner, mitigation plan, and status. Risks are identified during planning and continuously monitored at weekly syncs. Stakeholder communication uses a single source of truth (project README or release documentation) and follows templates for weekly status updates and incident communication. Escalation paths are clearly defined (Team-level → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor) with specialized protocols for security incidents.
